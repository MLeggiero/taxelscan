## The front end, which is the whole question

**A transimpedance amplifier is the wrong answer here**, and it is worth
recording why so nobody re-derives it. A TIA is the textbook response to a
megohm source, and it makes settling independent of node capacitance. But
rev-1's best property is that unselected rows are *actively driven low*, so
sneak paths terminate at a low impedance instead of floating — and that only
holds while the sense node also sits near ground. A TIA holds its summing node
at mid-rail, and then all 31 unselected crossings in the selected column inject
~1.65 µA each against a 1.65 µA signal. **The TIA breaks the single best thing
about the design it is supposed to improve.** On a single 3.3 V supply it would
also need a negative rail to have output room.

So the divider stays. The real design variable is the pulldown, and it trades
three things at once:

| `R1/R2` | V at rest (1 MΩ) | V pressed (50 kΩ) | 16-bit settle | Sneak share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.3 kΩ — rev-1's value | 9.8 mV | 186 mV | 2.3 µs | 9% |
| **10 kΩ — fitted here** | 24.9 mV | 435 mV | 5.9 µs | 24% |
| 33 kΩ | 53 mV | 830 mV | 25 µs | 50% |
| 1 MΩ — "match the sensor" | 1.60 V | 3.14 V | 389 µs | 97%, ≈2.5 fps |

Settling assumes ~70 pF at the mux common (CD74HC4067 C<sub>off</sub> × 16,
trace, amplifier input) and 11.1 τ to a 16-bit floor. "Sneak share" is the 31
unselected crossings — 1 MΩ/31 = 32.3 kΩ — as a fraction of the total shunt at
the node.

**Keep the pulldown low and put the amplitude back with gain.** The gain sits
*after* the high-impedance node, so it costs nothing in settling time, which is
what lets the pulldown stay small and the sneak share stay low. Amplifier
offset and mux leakage are amplified with the signal, but both are static and
the dark reference subtracts them every frame.

## Audit against rev-1

Everything rev-1 depended on was checked mechanically rather than by reading,
and all of it survives:

| rev-1 property | rev-3 |
|---|---|
| Unselected rows actively driven low, so sneak paths terminate at low impedance | **32/32** rows reach a 595 *output* pin |
| Row drivers that hold their level under load | same `SN74LVC595A` |
| `MUX_S0..S3` contiguous, so `scan.h` sets all four with one store | GPIO 4,5,6,7 |
| 33R damping on `ROW_CLK` / `ROW_LATCH` | `R3`/`R4` unchanged at 33R |
| The sense node carries nothing that loads it | `SENSE_A` = {R1, U5, U7} and `SENSE_B` = {R2, U6, U7}, exactly |
| 32 columns split 0–15 / 16–31 across two muxes | intact, and asserted |
| Four layers, dedicated ground and power planes | `In1.Cu` GND, `In2.Cu` +3.3 V, both matched to the outline |
| Live dark reference valid under load | unchanged — and it now has *less* to remove, see VREF below |

Three things are better than rev-1 rather than merely preserved: the reference
is ratiometric so rail movement cancels in hardware instead of in firmware; the
connector shells are grounded instead of floating; and the converter is 16-bit.

### What the audit found wrong

**The converter had no charge reservoir.** An SAR samples onto a switched
capacitor, and closing that switch throws charge back at whatever drives it; at
16 bits it has to settle before conversion starts. The op-amp was driving
`U8`'s sampling input directly. `../v2-module/README.md` had already specified
the fix for the hub — *"terminate each sense line with 1 nF C0G as a charge
reservoir for the ADC sample-and-hold: 51 Ω × 1.05 nF is 54 ns"* — and rev-3
had dropped it. Added as `R21`/`R22` (51R) into `C31`/`C32` (1 nF C0G), with
the resistor **outside** the feedback loop so it costs no accuracy, and a check
that the amplifier output cannot appear on the ADC net.

**Three rails had bulk and no high-frequency path.** `VCORE` fed four DVDD pins
from a 4.7 µF alone; `VREF` — the reference for a 16-bit converter — had only
its 10 µF; `+5V` had only the buck's 22 µF. Added `C24`/`C25`, `C26` (C0G) and
`C27`. There is now a check that every rail feeding a chip has at least one
100 nF on it.

**The plane zones were the wrong size.** Both were still 49 × 65 mm from the
generated outline, ~29 mm taller than the board they sit on. KiCad clips a fill
to the edge so the copper was right, but the polygon was a trap for the next
person to edit. `sync_pcb.py` now reshapes zones to the outline.

### The board is full, and the decoupling is where the space was

This is the most important thing in this file, and it is a **placement**
finding, not a schematic one. Measured, not estimated — every IC supply pin on
the board against the nearest 100 nF on its own rail:

| pin | rail | nearest cap |
|---|---|---|
| `U1.16` | ROW_VCC | **21.08 mm** |
| `U2.16` | ROW_VCC | **17.33 mm** |
| `U3.16` | ROW_VCC | **20.58 mm** |
| `U4.16` | ROW_VCC | **22.35 mm** |
| `U9.6` | VCORE | **13.87 mm** |
| `U9.23` | VCORE | 9.49 mm |
| `U9.39` | VCORE | 7.62 mm |
| `U9.49` | +3.3V | 9.41 mm |
| `U9.53`, `U9.54` | +3.3V | 9.12, 8.77 mm |
| `U6.24` | +3.3V | 10.12 mm |
| … | | |

**22 of 23 IC supply pins have no 100 nF within 2 mm.** The four 595 rail caps
sit at the bottom of the board while the parts they serve are at the top. The
RP2354A's core rail — the one with real di/dt on it — is decoupled at 7.6 to
13.9 mm.

A decoupling capacitor works by being a lower-impedance path than the loop back
to the bulk. At 10 mm of 0.15 mm trace the loop is ~10 nH, which is ~63 Ω at
100 MHz; the capacitor's own impedance at that frequency is milliohms. The
inductance is the circuit. **These parts are in the BOM and in the netlist and
are not decoupling anything.**

Why it happened is measurable too: the board is **78.8% courtyard density** on
1764 mm². Asked to find the nearest position to each MCU supply pin that clears
both the other courtyards and the 1589 tracks already routed, `place_new.py`
comes back with **3.8 mm at best and 14.7 mm at worst**. There is exactly one
free 3.4 × 7.8 mm block on the whole board and it is the one `JP1`–`JP3`
already occupy — the wall standing 0.55 mm off U9's entire right edge, which is
the edge carrying `ADC_AVDD`, a `DVDD` and two `IOVDD` pins.

So the four caps added here (`C33`–`C36`) do not fix this. Only `C33` landed
close enough to work. **The fix is space**, and there are three ways to buy it:

1. **Grow the board.** 49 × 39 mm is +3 mm and 1911 mm² — still 20% smaller
   than rev-1's 2392 mm², and density drops to 72.7%. Move `JP1`–`JP3` into the
   new strip and U9's right edge opens up. This is the cheapest fix and it
   costs nothing but panel area.
2. **Bottom-side 0402s for decoupling only.** Textbook: the cap goes directly
   under its pin with two vias. It was ruled out for this board, and it is the
   reason to revisit that.
3. **Accept it**, on the argument that at 1 MΩ a driven row sources 106 µA and
   the 595s have almost no dynamic current. That argument holds for `ROW_VCC`.
   It does not hold for `VCORE`, and the RP2350's core regulator is a buck.

### Passive grades, and which ones actually matter

Not every part needs to be good. These do, and the reason is specific:

| Part | Grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `R1`, `R2` sense pulldowns | **thin film, 0.1%, 25 ppm/°C** | They set the transfer function. A tempco *mismatch* between R1 and R2 is a bank-to-bank **gain** drift, and the dark reference removes offset, not gain — so this is the one error the firmware cannot see. |
| `R6`–`R9` gain network | **thin film, 0.1%, 25 ppm/°C** | G = 1 + Rf/Rg per bank; a mismatch between the two banks is again a gain error. Same lot for all four if possible, so the banks track. |
| `C20`, `C21` crystal load | **C0G/NP0, mandatory** | An X7R here moves the USB clock with temperature. |
| `C26` VREF | **10 nF C0G** | The reference for a 16-bit converter; 100 nF C0G is not an 0402 part. |
| `C31`, `C32` ADC reservoir | **C0G** | It supplies the sampling charge; a lossy dielectric puts that loss in the signal path. |
| `C22`, `C23` buck | **X7R, ≥25 V** | A 6.3 V-rated 22 µF 0805 is under half its marked value at 5 V bias — a different regulator than the one the compensation was chosen for. |
| All decoupling | **X7R, 25 V** | Never X5R, never Y5V. The voltage rating is for DC-bias headroom, not for survival. |

Everything else — `R3`/`R4` damping, `R10`, the terminators, the dividers, the
pull-ups — is ordinary 1% thick film and there is no reason to pay for more.

Unchanged and deliberately so: the SN74LVC595A row drivers, the 32-way FFC
connectors and their pinout, R3/R4, and the entire row and column fanout —
which is why rev-1's routed geometry can be transferred rather than redrawn.

### The connector shells are grounded

`J1`–`J4` use the `_MountingPin` variants of their symbols, so the shell is a
pin and it goes to `GND`. The plain `Conn_01x*` symbols have no pin for the
mounting pads the FH12 and JST GH footprints carry, so KiCad reported *"No net
found for component J1 pad MP"* twelve times and left all four shells floating.
`../v2-module/README.md` logged the same complaint and called it worth fixing
in the symbol.

On this board it is more than tidiness. A floating shell sits directly over 32
column lines at megohm impedance — an antenna, on the board whose entire reason
for existing is that the previous design picked up too much. Grounded, it is a
shield instead.

### Why the mux does not change

The CD74HC4067's ±1 µA worst-case leakage looks alarming against a 3.3 µA
signal. It is not, because the mux sits at the **low-impedance** end of the
chain: 100 nA into a 7.6 kΩ node is 760 µV. And `scan.h` names mux leakage as
one of the things the dark reference exists to subtract — it is measured every
frame, with no row driven, on every channel. $0.56, in LCSC stock, and rev-1's
proven mux placement and fanout transfer unchanged.

**If bring-up disagrees**, the fallback is an ADG706 (16:1, 1.8–5.5 V, 100 pA,
TSSOP-28, LCSC C408400). It is not pin-compatible, so that is a re-layout
rather than a swap — which is the reason to settle the question in phase 2, on
rev-1 hardware, before any of this is committed to copper.

### VREF is tapped off ROW_VCC, on purpose

The LTC1865L in MSOP-10 has a separate `VREF` pin, and it is fed from
**ROW_VCC through R10/C10** rather than from a precision reference. ROW_VCC is
also what the row drivers drive the matrix with, so the reference and the
excitation move together and the code becomes

    (Rpd_eff / (Rs + Rpd_eff)) * G * 65536

with the rail cancelled out of it entirely. That deletes a part *and* deletes
rev-1's rail-sag correction — `telem.railRaw` and the `ADC_RAIL` monitor exist
precisely to compensate for something that now cancels in hardware. R10/C10
keep the reference quiet at high frequency while letting it track at DC.

This is why `R5`, the 0 Ω link from +3.3 V to ROW_VCC, is **kept** where the v2
module removed it: it is the tap point.

## The bus

RS-485, half duplex, multidrop, polled by a master. Six conductors:

| Pin | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | +5 V | GND | DATA+ | DATA− | SYNC+ | SYNC− |

`J3` and `J4` are wired **identically**, so the bus and the power pass straight
through a board rather than terminating in it. Pull one board out and the
harness is broken — that is the honest failure mode, and it is obvious on a
bench. `R11`/`R12` are termination positions, fitted only on the two end
boards.

**Two pairs, not one.** The design's core property is that every mat samples
the same instant. `U11` is a second transceiver on a master-driven broadcast
pair. A board that could drive the frame-start broadcast is a board that could
desynchronise every other board on the harness, so driving it takes a
deliberate act of firmware:
- `DI` is strapped to GND, so a driven pair can only ever pull SYNC low.
- `~RE` is strapped on, so every board listens, the master included. The master
  times its own scan off the edge it receives, with the same delay as everyone
  else.
- `DE` sits on GPIO13 (`SYNC_DE`), held low by `R37` (10 kΩ). A board in reset,
  a blank board and every non-master board is receive-only.

The master pulses `SYNC_DE` high to send the frame-start, and each board takes a
falling-edge interrupt on `SYNC_OUT`. Until 17 September `DE` was strapped to
GND too, which left no board able to drive the pair once `USB_POWER.md` made
the master one of these boards. A single-ended sync line over a 4 m harness is
the noise problem this whole pivot exists to avoid.

`U10`'s `DE` and `~RE` are tied to one GPIO. Split across two, a firmware slip
leaves the driver enabled, which takes the whole harness down rather than one
board.

### Bandwidth

`emitBinV2` ships `rows*cols int16` plus contacts plus a trailer — at 32×32
that is **2180 bytes per frame**. At 20 Mbaud 8N1, derated 80% for turnaround,
the bus carries ~1.6 MB/s:

| Payload | Per board @ 80 fps | Eight boards |
|---|---|---|
| Contacts + telemetry, 132 B | 10.6 kB/s | 85 kB/s — **5% of the bus** |
| Conditioned 8-bit map, ~1150 B | 92 kB/s | 736 kB/s — 46% |
| Full 16-bit map, 2180 B | 174 kB/s | 1.39 MB/s — 87% |

At the 10 Mbaud fallback eight full maps do **not** fit. That is why the
payload is host-selectable per board: stream contacts from everything, promote
a board to full map on demand. `FF_GATED` and `FF_RAW` already exist in
`protocol.h`.

RS-485's rule of thumb — baud × length ≤ 10⁸ — gives 5 m at 20 Mbaud and 10 m
at 10 Mbaud. Eight boards at 0.5 m spacing is 4 m.

`JP1`–`JP3` set a 3-bit board address, so eight boards share a harness. Each
jumper straps its `ADDR` line to ground, and the MCU holds the line up
internally, so a **closed** jumper reads 0 and the firmware must enable the
internal pull-ups. The footprint is the *bridged* variant, so boards arrive from
the fab with all three straps closed — every board is address 0 until two of the
three bridges are cut. Cutting, rather than soldering, is deliberate: the
address stays readable by eye. (This replaced a DIP switch, `SW2`; see the
placement table below.)

## Power

The master can now supply the chain from its computer USB connection through
a TPS2553 current limiter and a reverse-blocking Schottky diode. A TUSB320LAI
detects the source's default, 1.5 A, or 3 A advertisement. The firmware selects
a nominal **321 mA downstream limit** after a default-current USB connection
has configured for 500 mA, or **632 mA** when CC advertises 1.5 A or 3 A.
The master's own load is additional; the low setting reserves up to 100 mA
for it. Fewer boards simply draw less current.

The earlier 310 mA estimate for eight boards was a preliminary average-load
estimate, not a guaranteed maximum. Startup, RS485 transmission, MCU activity,
conversion loss, and harness voltage drop must be measured before committing
to eight boards on a default-current computer port. Use 26 AWG power/ground
conductors with the JST GH connectors. See [USB_POWER.md](USB_POWER.md) for
calculated limits, wiring, firmware integration, and bring-up criteria.

## Historical placement notes

The current PCB is **54 × 36 mm**. The dimensions below document earlier
placement iterations and do not describe the current board.

**The board is 50 × 66 mm. Earlier drafts of this file claimed 48 × 28 mm and
a 44% saving against rev-1. That was wrong, and wrong for one reason worth
recording: the component-area estimate behind it used package BODY sizes where
what actually has to fit is the COURTYARD.**

A courtyard is roughly twice the body area on small SMD parts, and the error
was systematic across every line:

| Part | Claimed | Actual courtyard |
|---|---|---|
| CD74HC4067 SSOP-24 | 43.5 mm² | **81.8** |
| SN74LVC595A TSSOP-16 | 22.0 mm² | **42.4** |
| RP2354A QFN-60 | 49 mm² | **67.2** |
| SN65HVD75 SOIC-8 | 20 mm² | **40.0** |
| USB-C receptacle | 63 mm² | **100.2** |
| JST GH 6-way | 40 mm² | **76.5** |

`gen_pcb.py` computes every courtyard from the footprint files and totals them:
**1441 mm²** as first drawn. rev-1's own parts total ~1182 mm² in 2392 mm², so
rev-1 runs at **49% courtyard density**.

### Where it actually landed

The generator's band packing is safe but wasteful — it produced 3300 mm², 38%
*larger* than rev-1. **Hand placement did far better: 49 × 36 = 1764 mm², 26%
smaller than rev-1**, at about 78% courtyard density. That is dense; there is
not much room left for routing and it is worth expecting to give some back.

Two parts were then downsized, which is what took it further:

| Part | Was | Now | Saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SW1` BOOTSEL | TL3342, 8.5 × 6.0 | Omron B3U-1000P, 4.8 × 3.3 | **35 mm²** |
| `SW2` → `JP1-JP3` | DIP switch, 9.4 × 6.0 | 3 × solder jumper | **32 mm²** |

Courtyard total falls to **1373 mm²**, which is what let the outline come
down to **49 × 36 mm**.

**`J6` stays a 2.54 mm pin header.** It was briefly swapped for four test pads
to save 24 mm², and swapped back: a debug port you can plug a probe cable or
jumper wires into is worth 24 mm², and a pogo jig is not a thing you want to
find you need at three in the morning. A 1.27 mm 1×4 header would cost 18.9 mm²
instead of 39.5 if that trade ever looks different, but it needs a matching
cable.

**Does the address switch need to be a switch?** No. The *address* is needed —
eight boards share one RS-485 bus and each must answer to one number — but a
DIP switch is only one way to set it. Three solder jumpers cost 32 mm² less and
keep the address **readable by eye**, which matters when the failure being
debugged is two nodes answering at once. Storing it in flash and setting it over
USB would save all 57 mm² and cost nothing in parts, but then a replacement
board joins the harness with whatever address it was last given and collides
silently. The jumpers keep the answer on the board.

One lever was priced and rejected: swapping `J3`/`J4` from JST GH (1.25 mm) to
JST SH (1.0 mm) saves only 24 mm², because the horizontal SH footprint is
nearly as wide. Not worth a less rugged connector on the one part of the board
that gets plugged and unplugged.

The remaining lever is bottom-side placement, worth ~600 mm², and it is ruled
out: this is a single-sided assembly on purpose.

**Why rev-3 is not dramatically smaller than rev-1**: it reads one mat, as
rev-1 did, but it also carries an RS-485 bus, a second transceiver for sync, a
USB-C port, a buck regulator and a 16-bit converter that rev-1 did not have.
Dropping the XIAO module for bare silicon saves 385 → 67 mm²; the bus, power
and USB sections add most of that back.

Four layers: F.Cu signal, In1 unbroken ground plane, In2 3.3 V, B.Cu signal —
routing only, no bottom-side components.

## Verify

`./gen_rev3.py` asserts the whole design — **139 nets, 111 parts, 501
connections** — and `./check_faults.py` then breaks the generator on purpose to
show the assertions bite. **All 33 injected faults are caught** — the 34th case
is the unmodified baseline, which must report nothing. The table below is a
sample of them (counts re-run 17 September 2026, when the reversed-L1 probe was
added):

| Injected fault | Caught as |
|---|---|
| The op-amp left as a unity-gain follower | `bank A: inverting input is on AMP_A` |
| Feedback and gain-set resistors swapped | `bank A: R6 is not the feedback resistor` |
| VREF tied to +3.3 V instead of tapped off ROW_VCC | `VREF is not filtered off ROW_VCC through R10` |
| The two ADC channels crossed | `ADC CH0 is not on bank A` |
| A column moved to the other mux | `COL_16 is not on U6 - the splitter's bank assumption breaks` |
| A column dropped from the mux | `expected 32 COL_n nets, got 31` |
| The transceiver's DE and ~RE split onto two nets | `the data transceiver's DE and ~RE are not tied` |
| The sync driver's pull-down removed, so a blank board can drive the pair | `the sync driver's DE has no pull-down (<= 10k) to GND` |
| The harness data pair crossed between connectors | `J3 and J4 differ - the bus does not pass through` |
| USB VBUS taken straight to +5 V | `+5V_USB carries ['D2'], want ['D2', 'J5']` |
| One MCU supply pin left off the plane | `MCU DVDD pin(s) ['23'] are not on VCORE` |
| A mux select moved off the contiguous run | `MUX_S0..S3 are on GPIO [4, 5, 12, 7] - not contiguous` |
| The core rail fed from +3.3 V | `the core regulator has no inductor between VREG_LX and DVDD` |
| A QSPI pin wired, though bonded to the stacked flash | `QSPI_SD0 is wired, but it is bonded to the stacked flash` |
| The buck's FB tied to its output | `the buck's FB is tied to its output - it will regulate to the reference` |
| A connector shell left floating | `J4's shell is not grounded` |
| A footprint name in no library | `U8: footprint ... is in no library` |
| A pin name misspelt | `U8 (Analog_ADC:LTC1865L-MS) has no pin named 'V_{REFF}'` |

Two of those are worth singling out.

**The USB back-drive check was written wrong the first time.** It compared the
node sets of `+5V_USB` and `+5V` and asserted they did not intersect — but
moving VBUS onto `+5V` leaves the two nets sharing no *node* while shorting the
two supplies together, so the check passed the fault happily. Writing the
fault is what exposed it. It now names who is allowed on each rail instead.

**The buck's FB was genuinely wired wrong**, straight to +3.3 V. The TLV62569
is an adjustable part: FB servos to an internal reference, so tying it to the
output regulates the rail down to ~0.6 V and browns out the board — a fault
that presents as a dead regulator rather than a wiring error. R18/R19 are the
divider, and there is now a check and an injected fault for it.

### The one rule this file does not bend

Pin numbers are **looked up by name** from KiCad's own symbol libraries, never
typed: `p("U8", "V_{REF}")` returns the real pin 10 of the MSOP-10, and
`p("U8", "V_{REFF}")` stops the generator. Every part on this board has a stock
KiCad 10 symbol, so unlike `../v2-module/` there is no synthesised symbol
anywhere and no exception to the rule.

One substitution, and it is the one this repo already makes. rev-1 orders an
`SN74LVC595A` against a `74HC595D` symbol; here an `SN65HVD75DR` is ordered
against a `MAX3485` symbol. Both are the industry-standard 8-pin half-duplex
RS-485 pinout — RO / ~RE / DE / DI / GND / A / B / VCC — so the symbol and the
part cannot disagree about a pin without disagreeing about the whole family.

## The schematic

`rev3.kicad_sch` is generated too, by `./gen_schematic.py`, and unlike
`../v2-module/` it is **built from the netlist rather than transformed from
rev-1's drawing** — more than half this sheet is new symbols, so there was no
proven drawing to inherit. The sheet is label-driven: every pin with a net has
a global label sitting on it, and a label on a pin is the connection, so there
are no wires. That makes the drawing a mechanical rendering of `rev3.net`
rather than a second artifact to keep in step.

It is written in **KiCad 7 format on purpose**: kiutils writes it natively,
kicad-cli loads it and exports a netlist to check, and KiCad 8+ upgrades it on
the way in.

    nets   121 of 121 match rev3.net
    parts  69 on the sheet, 0 disagree with BOM.csv
    ERC    0 errors, 7 warnings
    nets*  121 of 121 still match after upgrade to KiCad 10
    ERC*   0 errors, 9 warnings after upgrade

**The last two lines are there because of a bug they caught.** The sheet is
verified in the format it is stored in *and* in the format KiCad actually
opens, and those are not equivalent: an earlier version matched all 121 nets in
KiCad 7 while the same file upgraded reported six `different_unit_net` errors.
All three units of the dual op-amp had collapsed onto unit 1, because KiCad 7
keeps a symbol's unit in an `instances` block that was never written, and the
upgrade rebuilt it from nothing. A check that only looks at the format nobody
opens is not a check.

The seven remaining warnings are all `lib_symbol_mismatch`, on the parts whose
symbols use `(extends)` — TLV9062, MAX3485, RP2354A — plus Crystal_GND24.
They are the cost of writing KiCad 7: the version 10 library format carries
fields (`exclude_from_sim`, `in_pos_files`, `do_not_autoplace`) that version 7
has no place for, so the sheet's cached copy differs from the library's. Pins,
numbers and positions are identical, which is what the 121-net match proves.

## The PCB

`rev3.kicad_pcb` is generated by `./gen_pcb.py`: outline, placement, nets and
both planes. **It is not routed** — that is interactive work in pcbnew, and a
generator emitting 400 tracks nobody had looked at would be worse than one
emitting none.

    pads   399 of 399 carry the net rev3.net gives them
    parity 0 genuine violations; 18 pads deliberately left unconnected
    DRC    389 violations, 279 of them unconnected items

`--schematic-parity` is the one that matters: KiCad comparing the board against
`rev3.kicad_sch` directly, so the board and the verified design are the same
circuit rather than two things that resemble each other. The 18 deliberate
pads are the stacked-flash QSPI bus, the spare GPIO, U4's carry-out and
USB-C's sideband pair; the check counts them separately so a real conflict
cannot hide among them.

Written as text rather than through kiutils, because kiutils cannot **read** a
KiCad 10 board at all — a pad's `(net "GND")` carries no number in this format
and its parser walks off the end of the list.

### Once the board has been placed by hand, the generator stands down

`gen_pcb.py` **refuses to overwrite a board that pcbnew has saved.** KiCad
stamps its own name into `(generator ...)`, which is the tell. Placement past
the first pass is human work — the hand-placed layout is 42% smaller than the
generated one — and there is no way to feed that back into `PLACE`, so the
generated version must not be allowed to win by accident.

To pick up netlist or footprint changes while keeping the placement, use
**Tools → Update PCB from Schematic** in pcbnew. `./gen_pcb.py --force` throws
the placement away and starts over.

The checks still run either way, so `./gen_pcb.py` on a hand-placed board is
the way to ask "does this still match the schematic?"

Two things this file learned the hard way:

- **Anchors are packed by band, not typed as coordinates.** A hand-written
  table of 69 positions had 88 courtyard overlaps in it, for the same
  body-versus-courtyard reason the size estimate was wrong. Bands are packed
  from real courtyard widths, so anchor collisions are impossible by
  construction; the passives are then nudged outward from a hint until they
  land somewhere free.
- **A pad's rotation is absolute, not relative to its footprint.** Setting only
  the footprint's `(at x y rot)` moves the pads but leaves each pad's *shape*
  pointing the original way, so a rotated SSOP-24's 1.9 mm pads end up
  broadside to a 0.65 mm pitch. That was 104 `shorting_items`, 104
  `solder_mask_bridge` and 36 `clearance` errors, every one of them inside a
  single component.

`pcb_top.png` and `pcb_persp.png` are the renders; `rev3.pdf` is the sheet.

KiCad ships no 3D model for two of these parts, and they are the two most
visible ones — the QFN-60 and the USB-C receptacle — so the board rendered with
a hole where each should be. `MODEL_FIX` in `gen_pcb.py` points them at the
nearest body KiCad does ship: QFN-56 7x7 P0.4 (same body, same pitch, four
fewer pins) and the GCT USB4105, also a 16-pin top-mount horizontal. This is
the picture only; nothing in it reaches the netlist, the BOM or any fabrication
output. All 22 model paths now resolve.

The three solder jumpers have no model and should not — a jumper is copper.

## Routing

Hand placement, then `freerouting` (imported with `./import_ses.py`), then a
grid maze router with rip-up-and-reroute (`./maze_route.py`) and a repair pass
for orphaned copper (`./close_gaps.py`). Where it stands:

    tracks  1777   vias  270   (F.Cu 1144, In2.Cu 294, B.Cu 339)
    planes  GND 1453.8 mm2 of a 1764 mm2 board; +3.3V 966.6 mm2
    pads    439 of 439 carry the net rev3.net gives them
    parity  0 genuine violations; 18 pads deliberately left unconnected
    DRC     10 unconnected pads
            2 track_dangling, 20 silk_overlap, 20 silk_over_copper

Zero shorts, zero crossings, zero clearance violations, zero copper-to-edge
violations. The routing work took the board from **27 unconnected pads to 8**;
adding the eight parts the audit called for then took it back to **10**, and
the silkscreen warnings are all from those eight landing where there was room
rather than where they belong.

**This is not a board to assemble.** Ten pads are open, and — more importantly
— every capacitor added in that pass sits 10–15 mm from the pin it serves. The
*bare* board is finished and orderable, see "The manufacturing package"; the
assembly wants a placement pass first, and probably 3 mm more board. Both are
argued under "The board is full".

### Two real bugs the first routing pass had, both from the same cause

**freerouting used the ground plane as an ordinary signal layer.**
`gen_pcb.py` had declared `In1.Cu` and `In2.Cu` as `signal`, and the label in
quotes ("GND", "PWR") is cosmetic — the `signal` type is what KiCad exports to
Specctra, and freerouting read that as permission. The result: 300 signal
segments through the ground plane, 276 through the supply, and **zero plane
copper**. `SENSE_A`, `SENSE_B` and `GAIN_A` were among the nets buried between
the planes, which is exactly the outcome this whole pivot exists to avoid.

Fixed by retyping the layers — `In1.Cu` is now `power` (freerouting will not
route on it at all) and `In2.Cu` is `mixed` (still poured, but available for
signal routing where the board is too dense to avoid it) — and by
`reserve_planes.py`, which strips anything freerouting had already put on
`In1.Cu`, drops the analog nets (`SENSE_*`, `GAIN_*`, `AMP_*`, `ADC_*`, `VREF`)
off `In2.Cu`/`B.Cu`, and cleans up the vias and stub tracks that were left
serving nothing once that copper was gone.

**Once GND was off limits to route on, freerouting placed *zero* vias down to
it anyway.** This board is single-sided assembly, so every GND pad is SMD on
F.Cu — the only physical way to reach the buried plane is a via. freerouting
wired GND pads to each other with ordinary F.Cu/B.Cu tracks instead, producing
up to 55 disconnected copper islands, and never dropped a single via to tie
any of them to the plane.

`stitch_gnd.py` fixes this: one via per *island* (a connected group of GND
copper), not one per pad — many pads are already tied to their neighbours by a
track. An island is skipped if it already contains a through-hole pad (a
connector's mounting pin, which carries copper on every layer already).

    ./stitch_gnd.py GND            # or any other net
    ./stitch_gnd.py GND --apply

It went through three placement models before the current one, and the
failures are worth keeping because they generalise:

1. **Point-only legality** (does the via itself clear every pad/via/track)
   placed all 46 GND islands cleanly by its own check — and real DRC then
   found **15 `shorting_items`**, every one of them a stub 3–10 mm long
   crossing an unrelated net's track partway along its length while both of
   its own endpoints were individually clear. Checking only the destination
   point was not enough.
2. **Path-aware legality**, sampling the whole stub rather than just its
   endpoint, regressed to 20/46 — because the very first sample of any stub
   sits at the anchor pad's own position, which is also where that pad's
   *own* existing GND track ends, so every candidate looked like it
   immediately collided with itself. Fixed by excluding the target net's own
   copper from the crossing test: same-net contact is never a short, it is
   the plane doing its job.
3. With that fixed, path-aware legality placed **44 of 46 safely** with real
   DRC confirming zero new shorts. The remaining 2 needed the search ring
   pushed out to 20 mm to find anything at all, which is itself a sign those
   stubs were unsafe at that length in this density — they were left
   unplaced rather than forced.

One more fix earned by a real (if small) DRC hit: the script's own clearance
margin (0.127 mm, matching the board's rule block) was not enough — one via
landed 0.131 mm from a track against an effective 0.15 mm minimum somewhere
between the board rule and net-class defaults. Margin widened to 0.2 mm and
the whole batch regenerated from a clean baseline rather than patching the one
offender by hand.

**29 GND islands remained**, in the densest part of the board — the row-driver
band and the QFN-60's own pin ring — and no straight-line stub from any
candidate ring, tested out to 20 mm, cleared every other net's copper.

That conclusion was half right. The density is real, but the search was also
handicapped twice over, and both are now fixed:

- `all_pads()` modelled every pad as its **circumscribed circle**. A TSSOP pad
  is 1.5 × 0.4 mm, whose circumscribed circle is 1.55 mm across — four times
  the pad's real width — so on a 0.65 mm pitch part every candidate beside a
  pin read as "on top of the neighbouring pin". That one approximation
  rejected 243 of 624 candidates for U2.8 on its own.
- Pads on the **same net** were counted as obstacles. In a plane-stitched
  design a GND pad's nearest neighbour is nearly always another GND pad, and
  brushing one shorts nothing — it joins two bits of the same island.

With real rotated-rectangle geometry and same-net pads excluded, the honest
answer is that a *straight stub* is simply the wrong tool here.
`maze_route.py` routes around things, and took the same board from 29 islands
to the handful below.

### The clearance model was wrong, in a way that hid itself

The router modelled keep-out by dilating a bitmap: stamp each shape into
per-layer boolean masks, grow every obstacle by a disc, and route through what
is left. Two errors in that, both silent:

1. A cell is stamped only when its **centre** lands inside a shape, so the
   shape's real edge can lie up to one cell diagonal — 0.0707 mm at
   `GRID = 0.05` — beyond the outermost stamped cell.
2. `disc(r)` contains only **integer** offsets, so its reach along an axis is
   `floor(r)` cells, not `r`.

The second one is why tuning the first never worked. `CLEARANCE` was set to
0.22, judged too conservative, and dropped to 0.19 — and both come out as
`floor(5.9) = floor(5.3) = 5` cells, an identical 0.25 mm of reach. Two
different constants, byte-identical behaviour, and real DRC reporting
clearances of **0.102–0.144 mm against a 0.150 mm rule** either way.

Replaced with an **exact distance field**. Every obstacle here is convex — a
rotated rectangle, a capsule, a circle — so the distance from a cell centre to
it is exact, cheap, and vectorises over a small window; the minimum over all
shapes is the distance to copper. `free[l] = dist[l] >= TRACK_W/2 + RULE`, and
the rule is the rule. The straight run *between* two adjacent cell centres
needs no separate check, because for a convex shape the distance along a
segment is minimised at an endpoint.

It is also faster than what it replaced: a few hundred small windows instead
of ~400 shifts of a 700 000-cell array. And it gave back the 0.075 mm per side
that the margin had been eating, which on 0.65 mm pitch is the difference
between a ground pin having a way out and not.

### A pad's rotation is absolute — for the second time

`Board._collect` computed each pad's angle as `pad_angle - footprint_angle`.
KiCad pad angles are **absolute**: they already contain the footprint's
rotation. J4's mounting pad is 2.7 × 1.0 mm and the router modelled it
1.0 × 2.7, so it "reached" that island with a stitching via sitting 0.45 mm
off the pad's real edge, reported success, and KiCad called the via dangling.

This is the same trap that once left 104 pad *shapes* unrotated on this board,
one layer down. Both are now fixed and both are worth remembering: in this
file format, a pad's `(at x y rot)` rotation is not relative to anything.

### Rip-up and re-route, and why the unit is a net

Eight ground pins ended up in pockets the size of their own pad. On 0.65 mm
pitch the gap between neighbouring pads is 0.25 mm and a 0.15 mm track with
0.15 mm either side needs 0.45, so the only ways out are past the end of the
pin row — and on this board a fanout bus runs along exactly there. Something
has to move.

The first attempt ripped one **segment** at a time, cheapest first. It moved
nothing, and the reason is worth keeping: the walls are polylines, and their
shortest pieces are 0.1 mm bends. Twelve candidates tried per pad, twelve
failures, no diagnostic.

So there are two rip modes now, cheapest first:

- **locally** — cut only the span of the neighbouring net that passes the
  pocket, leaving exactly two loose ends, and rejoin those with one A* call.
- **entirely** — pull the whole net and route it again from its pads.

A rip is accepted only when **both halves succeed**; a re-route that fails is
rolled back completely, so a rip that cannot be put back leaves no trace.

**Order matters, and getting it wrong cost 53 shorts.** The escape route has
to be committed to the board model *before* the ripped net is re-routed. The
first version planned both against a board that contained neither, and they
crossed each other — 53 `shorting_items`, 29 `clearance`, 16 `tracks_crossing`
from a pass whose own checks were clean.

`NEVER_RIP` protects what a grid search has no way to reason about: the analog
chain, `ROW_VCC` (VREF is tapped off it, and IR drop between the divider and
its own reference is the thing the ratiometric tap exists to remove),
`VREG_LX` (the buck's switching node, where loop area is the specification),
the power rails, and the differential pairs.

### The QFN thermal pad, and what it collides with

`U9`'s pad 61 is the RP2354A's **only** ground connection: a 3.4 × 3.4 mm
exposed pad with, until now, a single via at its corner — and that via sat
0.25 mm off the pad's edge, connecting nothing. The whole chip returned
through it.

The paste is fine, which is worth saying because it looks alarming: the pad
itself carries `(layers "F.Cu" "F.Mask")` with no paste, and the paste is nine
separate 0.91 mm apertures on a 1.13 mm grid — standard KiCad windowpane
practice, ~64% coverage so the part does not float.

That 1.13 mm grid is also where the vias belong. **Only four of the nine fit.**
The other five land on a VCORE track that runs across B.Cu directly underneath
the chip, and the two cannot share the space:

- the thermal vias are not optional — they are the ground path;
- `VCORE` cannot be re-routed around them, because `U9.6`, `U9.39` and
  `U9.50` are sealed into their own pin fields exactly like the ground pins
  in "What is actually left".

The reason VCORE is under the chip at all is that **`L1` sits 14.2 mm from
`VREG_LX`**, at the opposite corner of U9, so the core rail has to cross the
whole package to reach its pins. With L1 against the top edge where it belongs,
VCORE never goes near the thermal pad and all nine vias fit. That move needs
space this outline does not have — the same conclusion as everywhere else on
this page.

### Two silent-failure bugs found while adding eight parts

Both belong in the same family as the vacuous checks elsewhere in this file:
neither raised anything, and both produced confidently wrong output.

**`footprints()` read every newly added part as sitting at the board origin.**
Its position regex demanded exactly two tabs of indentation, `gen_pcb.place()`
writes three, and when the match failed the function returned `(0.0, 0.0)`
rather than complaining. So `sync_pcb.py` placed eight parts correctly in the
file, and every tool that reads the board afterwards — `maze_route.py`,
`place_new.py`, the density audit, `stitch_gnd.py` — believed those parts were
100 mm away at the origin. The router then dropped vias on top of their real
pads (three `shorting_items` on C33 alone) and reported "endpoint cell blocked"
for pins it thought were off the board. Fixed both ends: the regex accepts one
to three tabs, `place()` matches the block's own indentation, and a footprint
with no readable position is now a hard error, because a board file that cannot
say where a part is, is broken.

**`sync_pcb.py` thought the +3.3V plane was missing.** Its check searched for
`(net_name "+3.3V")`, which is what pcbnew writes; this toolchain writes
`(net "+3.3V")`. On `--apply` it would have poured a *second* +3.3V zone on top
of the first — two overlapping zones on the same net and layer, which fills
without complaint and is invisible downstream. It now parses the zones properly
and compares (layer, net) pairs.

One more, in the same spirit: `SEED` originally carried only (x, y). The
positions had been verified for parts rotated 90°, and what got placed was
rotated 0° — a pad 0.64 mm tall verified in a gap, then placed 0.64 mm wide.
Fifteen fresh pads landed on other nets' tracks. `SEED` carries rotation now.

### DRC is the only source of truth about what is unconnected

Two things used to answer that question and both were wrong, in opposite
directions:

- `stitch_gnd.net_islands()` unions segment **endpoints**, so a pad joins the
  graph only when a segment ends exactly on the pad's centre. KiCad's
  connectivity is shape-based — a track landing anywhere on the pad counts.
  28 islands claimed against 16 gaps KiCad actually reported, and stitching
  the extra 12 would have filled the tight spots the real ones needed.
- `bridge_nets.PAIRS` was that list transcribed by hand from one DRC run, and
  then left behind by every edit since.

Both are gone. Every tool here now reads `kicad-cli pcb drc` on the current
board, cached on its mtime.

### `+3.3V` has a pour too, but a fragmented one

`In2.Cu` carries a **+3.3V zone**, so +3.3V is a plane net exactly like GND
and most of its gaps close by reaching the pour rather than by point-to-point
routing. With one difference that matters: In1.Cu's GND pour is a **single**
polygon, so any via reaches all of it, whereas the 248 segments routed on
In2.Cu cut the +3.3V pour into **eleven** — a fresh via there can land in a
fragment that connects to nothing. So GND may drop a new via; +3.3V has to
reach copper KiCad already considers connected.

### Half of "unconnected" was dead copper — `close_gaps.py`

Eight of the remaining items were not missing routing at all. They were tracks
on B.Cu, or on the In2.Cu plane layer, ending at **exactly** the same x,y as a
pad or track of the same net on F.Cu with no via between them — the scars of
vias deleted by hand and by `reserve_planes.py`'s orphan sweep while the
copper they served stayed put. Every one was also reported as
`track_dangling`.

Routing over them made it worse, and instructively so: `maze_route.py`
connected the *pads* to their pour, correctly, and each orphaned stub stayed
behind as a second island on the same net, so DRC reported it again.

    ./close_gaps.py                  # a via, a delete, or a rebuild
    ./close_gaps.py --apply
    ./close_gaps.py --rebuild COL_6  # also pull a net that is merely badly placed

Three repairs, in order of preference: a **via** at the junction where one is
legal; a **delete** of the stub where the net has a pour, since every pad
there is served by its plane; a **rebuild** of the whole net where it has not,
because deleting one segment would only strand the next.

Two things that had to be learned:

- **The unit is a connected component, not a segment.** Deleting one dangling
  segment exposes the next, and the first version walked a 2.45 mm stub off
  the board 0.05 mm at a time — twelve DRC passes for eight stubs. Copper that
  reaches no pad of its own net is dead whatever its shape.
- **A point-to-point route must start on the net's own copper.** Starting on
  whatever layer happened to be *free* at the pad's cell let COL_19 begin on
  the In2.Cu plane layer directly beneath U6 pad 6, on a layer the pad is not
  on, with no via. DRC called it dangling, `close_gaps.py` deleted it, and
  `maze_route.py` laid it again — the two tools took turns routing and
  deleting the same net until that was fixed.

### `COL_6` and the board edge — a decision, not a slip

`COL_6` ends on a 0.5 mm pitch FFC pin 1.5 mm from the right edge, and its
escape is sealed by its own neighbours' fanout. Measured directly, by routing
it against a sweep of edge rules:

| copper-to-edge | `COL_6` |
|---|---|
| 0.30 mm (KiCad's default rule) | no route |
| 0.24 mm | no route |
| **0.22 mm** | **routes** |
| 0.20 mm | routes |

So the choice is one dead sensor column against 0.08 mm of edge margin.
JLCPCB's minimum trace-to-outline for a **routed** edge — which this board has
— is 0.2 mm, so 0.22 keeps a little over their capability and nothing over
their recommendation. `rev3.kicad_pro`'s `min_copper_edge_clearance` is now
0.22 to match, because a board should be judged by the rule it was designed
to; leaving the rule at 0.3 and shipping copper at 0.22 anyway is the same
geometry with worse bookkeeping.

The earlier note in this file — *"COL_6 runs 0.205 mm from the right board
edge … it is the tightest thing on the board"* — described the same corner
before it was measured. It is now 0.22 mm by decision.

### What is actually left

Eight pads, and they are all the same problem: a pin sealed into a pocket the
size of its own pad, inside a fine-pitch part's own fanout.

| | why |
|---|---|
| `U2.13`, `U4.13` | the 595s' `OE`, tied to GND. On 0.65 mm pitch the gap between neighbouring pads is 0.25 mm; a 0.15 mm track with 0.15 mm either side needs 0.45. The only way out is past the end of the pin row, and the row fanout runs along exactly there. Measured escape corridor: **0.40 mm where 0.45 is needed.** |
| `U5.15` | same, on the 4067 |
| `ROW_CLK_MCU` (`R3.1` → `U9.4`) | one end reaches the whole board; U9 pad 4 is sealed into a 3.2 × 1.4 mm pocket inside the QFN-60's 0.4 mm pin ring |
| three `+3.3V` gaps | around the MCU, the buck and the gain stage — the densest square centimetre on the board |

A 0.6 mm via does not fit in a 0.65 mm pin field, and a 0.5 mm one does not
either: it needs 0.40 mm to the neighbouring pad's copper and the nearest
legal spot past the pad tip is 0.29 mm from the perpendicular bus. Nothing
short of moving copper fixes these, and `maze_route.py`'s rip-up already tried
twenty neighbouring nets per pad, both locally and whole.

**These want twenty minutes in pcbnew**, where a person can drag a trace
0.15 mm and see the result, or one small placement change to open the
row-driver band. That is the honest next step, and it is the last one before
assembly.

### The zones really are filled now

For most of this board's life the two zones carried an OUTLINE and no computed
copper - `filled_polygon` blocks: zero. Nothing in the text-surgery toolchain
can run a polygon fill, and `kicad-cli pcb drc --refill-zones` computes one in
memory and throws it away. That was invisible because DRC's answers were
right; only the saved file was empty.

`kicad_tools.py fill` fixes it properly, and the fills are now in the board:
**GND 1481 mm² of a 1764 mm² board, +3.3V 977 mm².** The gerber export also
passes `--check-zones`, so the planes would have reached the fab either way -
but the file is no longer lying about what is on it.

### The daisy-chain power trace: measured, and left alone

`+5V_BUS` passes through each board, so the one nearest the supply carries all
eight boards' current. `widen_power.py` exists to take it from 0.15 mm to
0.25 mm, and the answer is that it does not need to:

| width | capacity (IPC-2221, 1 oz, 10 °C) | drop across the chain | last board sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.15 mm — as routed** | **0.71 A** | **206 mV** | **4.79 V** |
| 0.25 mm | 1.00 A | 124 mV | 4.88 V |

Against a 400 mA requirement that is 1.8× thermal margin, and the TLV62569
regulates down to 2.5 V input, so 4.79 V is not close to anything. Widening
also costs five clearance violations on a board this dense. Left at 0.15 mm
deliberately, not by omission.

(`widen_power.py` had a bug worth recording: it wrote its trial board to a
temp directory, where KiCad could not find `rev3.kicad_pro` and fell back to
default design rules - which flagged all 199 existing 0.15 mm tracks as too
narrow and buried the real answer. KiCad locates the project by BASENAME, so
the trial has to be called `rev3.kicad_pcb` next to a copy of the project.)

## The manufacturing package

    ./make_fab.py

Writes `fab/`: gerbers (with `--check-zones`, so the planes are plotted from a
freshly computed fill), Excellon drill files with a map, a JLCPCB assembly BOM
and a CPL, and `rev3-gerbers.zip` to upload. The plane layers come out at
251 kB and 166 kB, which is the quick sanity check that they are not empty.

> **State on 17 September 2026 — this supersedes the rest of this section.**
> - Every BOM line has a verified LCSC number, and there are no unconnected
>   pads.
> - The files come in two assembly sets: middle boards without the R11/R12
>   terminators, and the two end boards with them.
> - `fab/ORDER-NOTES.txt` lists the JLCPCB options the board needs: Standard
>   PCBA and epoxy-filled, capped vias. It also lists what to check in the
>   placement preview.
> - See AUDIT.md section 7.3 for the review behind it. The text below is kept as
>   the record of the earlier state.

**The bare board and the assembly are two separate orders, and they are not in
the same state.**

The **gerbers and drill files are finished**. Nothing outstanding affects
fabrication — the remaining faults are connections, not geometry — and the
plan wants five bare boards first anyway, to seat a real mat tail on each
connector before any assembly job is released. That order can go today.

The **assembly cannot**, for two reasons:

1. **A handful of pads are still unconnected** (listed under "What is left"
   below). A board built like this is a board that does not work.
2. **Seven BOM lines have no LCSC number:** `U8` (LTC1865L), `L1`, `L2`,
   `J1`/`J2` (the FFC connector), and the three thin-film pairs `R1,R2`,
   `R6,R8`, `R7,R9` — deliberately blanked when the thin-film grade was
   specified, because the ordinary thick-film part number no longer applies.
   Each needs picking against LCSC stock on the day. (`JP1`–`JP3` are solder
   jumpers: copper, no part, correctly excluded rather than counted missing.)

   `make_fab.py` now treats a blank LCSC as a **hard stop** and writes
   `fab/NEEDS-PARTS.txt`. JLCPCB will accept an order with a blank part field
   and simply not place those designators, and the first sign of it is a board
   that does not work.

3. **Check every rotation against JLCPCB's own preview.** The CPL is emitted
   from KiCad's numbers with no correction applied, and their library's idea
   of zero disagrees often enough that a rotated 4067 is a scrapped board.
   This is a human step by design.

### Placement wants a human pass, still

Band packing guarantees nothing collides, but it does not honour the one rule
that matters most here: the muxes should sit directly inboard of J2, and in
the generated layout they are a band away. The sense node is the
high-impedance node on this board and its settling time is set by the
capacitance hanging off it, so that adjacency is worth reclaiming by hand.

Also outstanding: **1 `silk_overlap`** — R10's outline touches U2's, which is
cosmetic and does not affect the fab. That is the only DRC violation left on
the board.

Before any of it, two things have to happen on a bench:

1. **Measure the sensor.** One row, one column, a DMM, unpressed and pressed
   hard. Everything in the pulldown table is sized against an *assumed* 50 kΩ
   under firm press. This is the only genuinely blocking item.
2. **Prove the front end on rev-1 hardware.** Swap R1/R2, tack a gain stage on
   U7, hang a CNT mat on it. Real settling, real crosstalk and real mux leakage,
   on a board already known to work — before committing any layout.

### Confirm before the BOM freezes

Four values in `BOM.csv` are marked and are estimates or inferences, not
datasheet reads:

- **R1/R2 = 10 kΩ and R7/R9 = 2 kΩ** — both follow from the pressed resistance.
- **The TLV62569's FB reference**, assumed 0.6 V, which sets R18/R19.
- ~~**L1 = 3.3 µH**, the RP2350 core regulator inductor~~ — read on
  10 September 2026: the guide specifies the Abracon AOTA-B201610S3R3-101-T
  and an RC filter on VREG_AVDD; both are now in the design.
- ~~**C20/C21 = 15 pF** crystal load~~ — confirmed for the ABM8-272-T3
  (C<sub>L</sub> 10 pF) with the guide's 1 kΩ series resistor, now fitted.
- **The LTC1865L's supply range.** It is the 3 V part in a family whose other
  member is 5 V; that 3.3 V is inside its range is assumed, not verified.

And one behavioural assumption: that on the RP2354A the QSPI pins are bonded to
the stacked flash die and must be left unconnected, with only `~QSPI_SS`
brought out for BOOTSEL. The generator enforces it; the datasheet has not
confirmed it.
